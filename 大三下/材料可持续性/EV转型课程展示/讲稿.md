## 第 1 页

Hello everyone, my topic is designing a phased EV transition plan for Naples, drawing primarily on the experience of Xi'an.

## 第 2 页

Let me start by asking two questions: Why did Xi'an perform well? And What can Naples learn?

## 第 3 页

The focus in Xi'an is not on purchasing vehicles or installing charging stations in isolation, but on advancing charging infrastructure, vehicle fleets, platforms, and the industry as a whole.

## 第 4 页

Its logic is: policies provide certainty, high-mileage fleets transition first, charging follows, and then use data to improve the plan.

## 第 5 页

Naples already has a SUMP, a ZTL, and incentives for electric buses, but these have not yet been integrated into a comprehensive system.

## 第 6 页

Therefore, Naples should first integrate the fragmented infrastructure by phasing out old systems, building charging infrastructure, implementing data feedback, and providing transitional support.

## 第 7 页

The system diagram illustrates that governance, policy, charging, fleets, and air quality must be linked into a single chain of execution.

## 第 8 页

In terms of priorities, we should focus first on public transportation, taxis, tour buses, and port logistics, rather than advancing all vehicle types equally.

## 第 9 页

The roadmap calls for prioritizing public transportation and charging infrastructure first, followed by expansion into logistics, taxis, and tourism, and finally aligning with the EU 2035 targets.

## 第 10 页

The causal loop illustrates that charging convenience creates a positive feedback loop, but costs and charging demand also act as barriers.

## 第 11 页

Therefore, we need to monitor KPIs at every stage, such as bus reliability, charger uptime, and charging accessibility.

## 第 12 页

In terms of environmental benefits, replacing high-mileage vehicles first will have the greatest immediate impact on improving local air quality.

## 第 13 页

The final recommendation is: build the system first, then expand. Naples should first establish a reliable charging, data, and service infrastructure centered on a fleet of high-mileage, controllable, and publicly visible vehicles, and then promote the adoption of private vehicles.
